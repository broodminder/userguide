# Managing Nectar Flows with BroodMinder

Nectar flows are short, dynamic periods during which timing matters.

Knowing that a flow is coming, confirming when it actually starts, understanding how individual colonies are responding, and identifying the right moment to add supers or harvest can have a major impact on the final crop.

BroodMinder combines hive data, colony information and environmental indicators to help beekeepers manage this entire process — from anticipation to harvest.

---

## The Nectar Flow Management Cycle

**ANTICIPATE → CONFIRM → QUALIFY → FORECAST → ACT → OPTIMIZE**

Each step answers a different question:

- **ANTICIPATE** — When is the next nectar flow likely to begin?
- **CONFIRM** — Has the flow actually started?
- **QUALIFY** — Are my colonies responding as expected?
- **FORECAST** — How is the flow likely to develop?
- **ACT** — When should I add supers?
- **OPTIMIZE** — When is the flow ending and when should I harvest?

---

## 1. ANTICIPATE

### Know when the flow is coming

A nectar flow begins before significant weight gain appears on the scale.

The first step is therefore to understand what is happening around the apiary: flowering development, weather conditions and the potential of the available floral resources.

BroodMinder's **Nectar Flow Index (NFI)** helps visualize the expected evolution of nectar flow conditions.

Combined with flowering indicators, it provides an early indication that a potential production period is approaching.

### What to look for

- Increasing Nectar Flow Index
- Beginning of flowering
- 10% bloom
- 50% bloom
- Favorable weather conditions
- Expected duration of the flowering period

### Why it matters

Anticipating the flow gives the beekeeper time to prepare colonies and equipment before significant nectar intake begins.

The objective is not to react to weight gain after it happens, but to be ready when the opportunity starts.

> **Anticipate the opportunity before the nectar reaches the hive.**

---

## 2. CONFIRM

### See when the flow actually starts

Flowering does not automatically mean nectar production.

Once the expected flowering period begins, hive weight and productivity provide direct evidence of what the colonies are actually collecting.

Before the flow, daily productivity may remain around zero or even be slightly negative as colonies consume their stores.

When nectar intake begins, daily productivity turns positive and cumulative weight starts to rise.

This change provides a clear signal that the nectar flow has started.

### What to look for

- Daily productivity moving from negative to positive
- Sustained positive weight gain
- Change in the slope of cumulative production
- Increased foraging activity

### Key point

The **Nectar Flow Index anticipates** the opportunity.

**Hive productivity confirms it.**

> **The flowers tell you a flow may begin.  
> The hive tells you when it actually does.**

---

## 3. QUALIFY

### Put production into colony context

Not every colony responds to a nectar flow in the same way.

Two hives located next to each other can experience the same weather and floral resources while producing very different results.

This is why productivity should not be interpreted alone.

Combining **productivity data with colony development and brood information** helps explain differences between colonies.

### Example

Consider two colonies in the same apiary:

**Colony A**

- Strong brood development
- Approximately 95% brood level
- Strong foraging activity
- High productivity

**Colony B**

- Declining brood level
- Reduced colony strength
- Lower foraging activity
- Lower productivity

Both colonies experience the same nectar flow.

The difference comes from the colonies themselves.

### Why it matters

Production data tells you **what is happening**.

Colony data helps explain **why it is happening**.

This makes it possible to distinguish between:

- A weak nectar flow
- A colony-specific problem
- Insufficient colony strength
- Abnormal production behavior

> **Measure the flow. Understand the colony.**

---

## 4. FORECAST

### Know where production is — and where it is going

Once the nectar flow has started, the next question becomes:

**How long can it continue?**

Current productivity describes what the bees are collecting today.

The Nectar Flow Index provides additional context about the conditions expected over the following days.

Combining the two creates a more complete view:

**MEASURED PRODUCTION → TODAY → EXPECTED CONDITIONS**

### What to look for

- Current daily productivity
- Cumulative production
- Nectar Flow Index trend
- Expected flowering development
- Weather evolution
- Differences between measured and expected performance

### Example

A colony may currently be gaining several kilograms per day.

If the Nectar Flow Index remains high or continues increasing, favorable conditions may persist.

If the index begins declining, the beekeeper can anticipate a possible slowdown before it becomes fully visible in hive weight.

### Why it matters

Forecasting helps transform hive monitoring from a retrospective tool into a management tool.

Instead of only knowing what happened yesterday, the beekeeper can begin preparing for what may happen next.

> **Measure today. Prepare for tomorrow.**

---

## 5. ACT

### Know when it is time to add supers

During a strong nectar flow, colonies can gain several kilograms per day.

At this stage, available storage space becomes critical.

If additional supers are added too late, lack of space can limit the colony's ability to store incoming nectar.

BroodMinder productivity data makes rapid accumulation visible.

### What to look for

- Strong positive daily productivity
- Rapid cumulative weight gain
- Sustained nectar intake over several days
- Strong colony condition

When these signals align, the beekeeper can make an informed decision to add additional supers.

### From data to action

**Strong colony + sustained productivity + favorable outlook → ADD SUPER**

The objective is simple:

Give the colony enough storage capacity while the opportunity is still developing.

> **Don't wait until the hive is full to react.**

---

## 6. OPTIMIZE

### Catch the end of the flow

Knowing when a nectar flow is ending can be just as important as detecting when it starts.

As floral resources decline, daily productivity gradually decreases.

The cumulative production curve begins to flatten.

Eventually, daily gains approach zero or become negative again.

These signals indicate that the production phase is ending.

### What to look for

- Declining daily productivity
- Several days of decreasing gains
- Cumulative production reaching a plateau
- Falling Nectar Flow Index
- End of flowering
- Reduced foraging activity

### From data to action

**Declining flow + production plateau → PREPARE TO HARVEST**

Depending on the operation, the same information can also help determine when to:

- Remove supers
- Harvest honey
- Move colonies
- Prepare for the next floral resource
- Reorganize the apiary

### Why it matters

The goal is not simply to know how much honey was produced.

The goal is to identify the right moment to move from **production management to harvest management**.

> **Catch the end of the flow. Harvest at the right time.**

## From Anticipation to Harvest

Managing a nectar flow is not based on a single measurement.

It is a sequence of observations and decisions.

| Stage | Question | BroodMinder information | Possible action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ANTICIPATE** | Is a nectar flow approaching? | Nectar Flow Index + flowering | Prepare |
| **CONFIRM** | Has it actually started? | Daily productivity + weight | Validate the start |
| **QUALIFY** | Are colonies responding correctly? | Productivity + brood | Identify strong or weak colonies |
| **FORECAST** | Where is the flow going? | Productivity + Nectar Flow Index | Plan ahead |
| **ACT** | Do colonies need more space? | Strong cumulative gains | Add supers |
| **OPTIMIZE** | Is the flow ending? | Falling productivity + plateau | Harvest / move |
